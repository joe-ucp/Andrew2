# Candidate biology and discriminating follow-up

Reviewed 23 October 2026. `candidate_annotations.json` is the structured annotation source; its CSV companion has fifteen rows: the fourteen primary published lower-content overlaps and excluded NUPL1 as an explicitly separate illustration. This selective review adds external context to the catalog. It is not a systematic review or a therapeutic ranking. “Not located” means the bounded searches did not retrieve a qualifying independent study, not that no study exists.

The guide-FDR precision-loss finding changes the evidential description. The fourteen are overlaps with the authors' published insulin-content calls. Selected continuous S7 records support two paired positive FDR<0.1 guides per replicate for these fourteen and NUPL1. This is source-supported selected evidence, not an independent experimental replication or full-universe probability reconstruction. The binary S8 fields cannot confirm the nominal threshold; omitted S7 guides are not established nonsignificant, and no third-guide claim follows. External biology cannot repair lost precision or validate a joint inflammatory survival/function phenotype.

## What the independent beta-cell evidence adds

**SSR2** provides a concrete distinction between insulin supply and glucose responsiveness. Rat beta-cell depletion/knockout and rescue studies link SSR2 to proinsulin biosynthesis and stored insulin, while a robust stimulated fold response remains. Thus the relevant follow-up must show both absolute secretion and secretion relative to content. The inference that reduced ER demand might contribute to survival is a hypothesis, not an observed cytokine mechanism. [Huang et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1096/fj.202002774R).

**NCOA6** supports a different mechanism: transcriptional maintenance of beta-cell function. Candidate-targeted depletion affected glucose-responsive secretion in EndoC-betaH1, and conditional mouse deletion affected secretion and target-gene expression. The cell-line experiment normalized to content and the mouse manipulation began during development; neither establishes acute inflammatory protection. This candidate motivates identity/transcriptional measurements alongside secretion. [MLL3/MLL4–MAFA/MAFB study, 2015](https://doi.org/10.2337/db15-0281).

**CREB3** is a warning against treating survival direction as universal. Silencing sensitized rodent beta cells and human islet preparations to palmitate-induced apoptosis. That finding does not refute a cytokine-specific catalog result, but it rules out interpreting that result as general stress resistance. A cytokine/palmitate contrast would be informative after the basic matched-condition phenotype is reproduced. CREB3 is not interchangeable with CREB3L1, CREB3L2 or CREB3L3. [Cnop et al., 2014, Fig. 5](https://doi.org/10.2337/db13-1383).

**SLC8B1/NCLX** has a direct calcium-handling and secretion-timing rationale. Molecular suppression altered mitochondrial/cytosolic calcium signaling and delayed insulin secretion in rodent beta-cell experiments. A single terminal content measurement could miss the temporal defect. This favors time-resolved secretion with basal values, total output and viable-cell normalization; it does not predict a protective direction in human inflammation. [NCLX primary study, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0046649).

**UBE2C** adds a growth-versus-death alternative. A mouse beta-cell conditional-knockout study links Ube2c to compensatory proliferation and insulin sufficiency. Consequently, its relative screen representation should not be interpreted solely as resistance to death. Absolute live-cell counts, death markers and proliferation are required to distinguish those possibilities. [Jiang et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00018-023-04868-8).

These five have independent candidate-targeted beta-cell/islet functional studies in this review, with different models and endpoints. **RAD52** has beta-cell observational evidence: reduced protein in AGE2-treated INS-1 cells, without a targeted intervention demonstrating its role. **PEX13** has located beta-cell perturbation evidence in a doctoral thesis, flagged as such; it should not be counted as a peer-reviewed journal replication. [Lim et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19120318/), [Bonilla-Martinez thesis](https://jlupub.ub.uni-giessen.de/items/c229c6fe-a058-435d-9de9-c05c53e1f0fb).

## What remains indirect

For **C12orf29, EXOSC3, TM2D2, COA5, SLC2A11 and PDS5A**, the retrieved independent mechanism studies concern other systems. They support distinct process labels—RNA ligation, RNA surveillance, membrane/Notch-related biology in flies, respiratory-complex assembly, heterologous sugar transport and cohesin function—rather than a shared beta-cell mechanism. **ZNF616** remains a predicted transcriptional regulator. These annotations cannot establish insulin-specific substrates or pathways. The structured resource records primary or authoritative source URLs and a specific unresolved experiment for every row.

Useful falsifiable alternatives include broad biosynthetic suppression, altered mitochondrial or substrate metabolism, altered cell cycle and identity, changed release versus storage, and library/guide artifacts. These are proposed explanations, explicitly labeled in the resource. The list is not a pathway-enrichment result: fourteen selected candidates, uneven literature coverage and broad cellular functions make a post hoc unifying pathway narrative especially vulnerable to selection bias.

**NUPL1/NUP58** illustrates the separation of biology from eligibility. Its published lower-content call survives an exact-expression-symbol exclusion. NCBI identifies the synonym; human-islet proteomic work includes NUP58 without establishing a candidate-specific causal function. The completed, separately defined HGNC/Ensembl sensitivity maps NUPL1 to NUP58 (HGNC:20261; ENSG00000139496) and includes it among that sensitivity's 14 overlaps; SLC8B1 is excluded there because its source symbols produce a many-to-one collision. That comparison includes 12,826 genes and 306 survival-favoring targets, with conditional OR 1.1444 (95% CI 0.6124-1.9735), p=0.3537. The mapping ledger and result are recorded in `results/identifier_sensitivity/` and `evidence/identifier_mapping_sensitivity.json`; the locked exact-symbol primary remains unchanged. NUP58-specific perturbation/rescue and matched inflammatory viability/content/secretion remain untested. [Authoritative identity record](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/9818), [2026 original proteomic study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1535947626000848).

## Follow-up priorities

For mechanism discrimination, SSR2 and NCOA6 contrast biosynthetic supply with transcriptional function; CREB3 tests stressor dependence; SLC8B1 tests temporal secretion; UBE2C tests growth confounding. This is a rationale for a small validation panel, not an efficacy or safety ordering. Include at least one candidate with weaker external biological characterization if resources allow, so confirmation is not restricted to well-studied pathways.

The common proposed experiment is a perturbation-by-inflammation factorial design in matched batches, with independent targeting and rescue. Measure absolute viable-cell recovery, death and proliferation; insulin/proinsulin per viable cell and per culture; and absolute, viable-cell-normalized and content-normalized basal/glucose-stimulated secretion. Retain dynamic secretion where timing is plausible. Repeat in independent human donor or stem-cell preparations before claiming transportability. A preservation claim requires a specified margin and adequate precision, not failure to detect a difference. Full design and decision patterns are in `source_design_comparison.md`; no experiment has been performed here.

## Review scope and retrieval

Search families combined each source symbol with `beta cell`, `islet`, `insulin`, `knockout`, `silencing` and the known process/alias when appropriate. Synonym searches included NCLX, GLUT11 and NUP58. Selected hits were followed to original journal/PMC/PubMed or institutional records. Reviews and commercial gene summaries were leads only; process-only prediction is explicitly identified. The PEX13 thesis is retained as separately labeled primary gray literature. Abstract-only claims are confined to what the abstract supports. Indexed original full text or primary abstracts supplied some papers when original XML was unavailable. This review does not claim exhaustive candidate literature, independent joint-phenotype confirmation or external human specialist assessment.
